Challenges in the Dissemination of Knowledge

What Challenges are Raised by the Dissemination and/or Communication of Knowledge?

Communicated through objects

Essay · Sukira Harris · 4 min read

Object 1A Photo I Took of a Rhino in South Africa

I included this object as it is able to justify how the miscommunication between languages affects the dissemination of knowledge. My first object is a photo of a rhino taken on safari. This photo represents a story I was told about rhinos by my South African grandmother. Growing up I went to South Africa often to visit my family there and one of my favourite stories my grandma told was how the black rhino received its name. The story my grandma told was about how a lot of people misinterpret the rhino's name, believing that the black and white rhino were given their titles from their colour although this is not accurate, they are both grey. The miscommunication between the Dutch settlers in Africa and the English is what has led to these misleading names. Originally what we refer to as the white rhino the Dutch settlers referred to as the “wijde lip” (wide lip). The white rhinos received this name as their lips were much wider than ‘black’ rhinos as they have a pointier upper lip. When the dissemination of this name reached England the knowledge was misinterpreted into the word ‘white’. This misinterpretation of communication between the Dutch and English led to the ‘black’ rhino being given its name in order to differentiate it from the ‘white’ rhino. Although the name ‘white’ and ‘black’ rhino do not in fact help differentiate the species at all. Due to the miscommunication of knowledge through language barriers, it has led to the rise of incorrect knowledge. This incorrect knowledge is now universally accepted, leading to a large population of people obtaining incorrect information. In conclusion, misinterpretation of the dissemination of knowledge is affected by the language in which we use to communicate, therefore raising the challenge that we as knowers acquire incorrect knowledge. This knowledge becomes easily accepted without explanation due to the limitations of language.

Object 2Screenshot From a Video of Me Dancing

My second object is a screenshot of me dancing. The significance of this object is how it demonstrates the varieties of knowledge communities in society, affecting which means of knowledge we communicate through to one another. This photo represents the communication of language through dance. This screenshot was derived from a video of a dance which was expressing a story of someone who is struggling with mental illness. The aim of a story is to evoke emotion in the audience, in this specific case dance was a representation of anguish. The language of dance contains physical forms of communication such as body language and facial expressions, from an early age this is how we as humans express emotion. Therefore, the language of dance is able to evoke emotion through the physical ways in which we communicate. I was having a conversation with a friend about how dance can express a story. When I showed them this video, all they saw was a dance but not a story. This is when I realised that because they don't have the dance knowledge that qualified dancers obtain, they don't see a story, therefore they can’t be communicated knowledge through dance. To me dance is a way to express stories and advocate change. Although, because they aren't able to understand or interpret dance due to their lack of knowledge, they cannot be communicated the same knowledge. Therefore the dissemination of knowledge is affected by what knowledge the knower obtains. This in return raises a challenge as we are not able to present knowledge the same way to everyone, as knowledge creates certain communities and subsections. Since knowledge is restricted to certain communities, if you are not in a certain community you lack exclusive knowledge. Since we cannot use the same means of communication, this hinders us from obtaining certain knowledge due to knowledge barriers.

Object 3Screenshot from Influencer Instagram

My third object is a screenshot I have taken from an influencer’s Instagram page. This screenshot is about the effect that Instagram has on young people's lives through the dissemination of socially constructed knowledge. I chose a photo off of this Instagram, of someone representing what the fake, ideal, photoshopped version of themselves looks like vs. the real, authentic self. This manipulation via Photoshop is a substantial societal issue creating insecurity within our youth. Social media is able to manipulate the harmful dissemination of knowledge by choosing what they post and how they present it. Social media has a large impact on our society considering in 2019 there were already 5 billion mobile users (Clark). Within these users 72% of teens age 13-17 use Instagram (Clark). Meaning a large portion of teens are exposed to the construction of harmful knowledge communicated via Instagram every day. The majority of these teens rely on social media as a source of information, but they are subconsciously presented with false information. Manipulated knowledge on social media affects many young people's mental health as they attempt to deconstruct a media perpetuated standard. The caption of this Instagram post accentuates the effect of obtaining altered knowledge by saying how it, “Ruins your self confidence and then you're wanting to look like someone who just doesn't exist.” This caption highlights how the beauty standards have been magnified to an unrealistic and widely unachievable level leading to self-esteem issues in the youth in our society. The fake dissemination of knowledge is trying to be addressed by people such as this influencer by juxtaposing reality vs Photoshop. Photos contain the ability to disseminate a different perspective of knowledge on social media via manipulation of Photoshop, affecting the knowledge in which the youth in our society face. Therefore a challenge is raised by how easy it is to manipulate a photo to communicate manipulated knowledge, and how this knowledge becomes easily accepted by the youth.
