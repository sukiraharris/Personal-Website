Artists vs. Scientists

For Artists and Natural Scientists, which is more important: what can be explained or what cannot be explained?

Essay · Sukira Harris · 7 min read

What can be explained and what cannot be explained are both important roles towards the acquisition of knowledge. Although, each area of knowledge contains different definitions of what can and cannot be explained, such as the arts and natural sciences. In the arts, what can be explained is provided by the artist communicating a message in order to provoke a response in the audience. When an artist’s message is not clearly stated it shifts how the piece of art is perceived, allowing for more interpretative freedom amongst viewers. Although in some cases the artists’ intent is necessary for the viewers to feel the full impact of the piece. In contrast, scientists employ a systematic approach utilising observation and experimentation to study what can be explained in the physical world, to create new levels of understanding alongside theories. However, what cannot be explained allows the innovative discovery of new information, although it is not always necessary. This essay will argue that for natural scientists what can be explained is more important and for artists, what cannot be explained is more important due to the aforementioned reasons.

For artists, what cannot be explained is more important than what can be explained, as the unknown in art is what provides more creativity amongst viewers and critics. What can be explained in art is created by the artist’s intent for the viewer, but if the intent is not given it allows creative freedom for the viewers to come up with their own interpretation. An example is the short story “The River” by Flannery O’Connor which is about a young boy who lives with neglectful parents that give him a babysitter for the day. The babysitter takes him to a preacher and the boy experiences a religious awakening, but goes back to the river and drowns himself. The author did not provide their intent for why the story ended this way, allowing the rise of many debates among readers and critics with different perspectives on the story's ending. The purpose of art is to have an impact, therefore withholding intent allows the viewers to become more engaged by pondering what cannot be explained. For my English teacher, the ending of this story was interpreted as escaping a fate worse than death. My teacher believed that the bad mistreatment of the boy from his family was worse than death itself. However Nasrullah Mambrol, the literary critic perceived the ending to represent the Christian faith, since the boy was exposed to a religious awakening and drowned himself in the same river allowing Jesus to take his pain. These interpretations stem from O’Connor withholding her intent, allowing the readers not to be directed but to ponder what cannot be explained about the art, allowing freedom for more ideas to be shared and enhanced. For an artist to provide an intention it is not always important because art is dependent on the relationship between the artwork and the audience. Therefore, this story further implies that for artists what cannot be explained is more important than what can be explained to allow art to flourish, giving rise to more creativity among individuals.

Alternatively, what can be explained is more important for some artists who have a specific message to convey. An example of such art is ‘The watercolour of Terezin 1944’ by Peter Kien (15 influential political art pieces). At first glance, this piece does not have a powerful message, however, the context is what makes it remarkable. Kien was a Jewish prisoner in the concentration camps of World War II who created art to portray the inhumane conditions that the prisoners faced in the concentration camp, Terezin. This painting exacerbates the effect of the holocaust through many features, specifically the use of water paint. Water paint comes out as translucent at first and requires the application of multiple layers, symbolising how as viewers we can only see the surface of the pain that the prisoners felt. Still, there are many traumatic dark experiences that go back in history with it. This piece of art highlights that the context of art will transform the power and effect it holds. Without the context of the concentration camp being provided, this piece is only looked at from a surface level rather than understanding the truly significant meaning behind it. Therefore, for some artists, it is essential for the context of the piece to be provided in order for the viewer to comprehend its impact. The power Kien's art holds is within the context, without it the piece lacks meaning and purpose. Therefore, for art with a specific purpose or intention what can be explained is more important than what cannot for the viewer to understand the piece of work.

In contrast to the arts, for natural scientists, what can be explained is more important and is identified through the scientific method by utilising evidence and observation to justify theories, although these theories cannot be reliable or further applied to the natural world without the important ability to explain them. I applied the scientific method when conducting my biology internal assessment which was “To what extent does changing the colour of wavelength affect the growth in mung beans”. I found that green wavelengths do in fact have an impact on the growth of plants contrary to popular belief due to the variety of pigments in plants. What can be explained about the findings of my experiment are important to identify in order to further apply the results in the biological field to their full potential. If my research could not be explained then these results cannot be justified, reducing the overall applicability of my experiment. Therefore, in order for natural scientists to produce and create new and accurate information, the theory must be explained both strongly and cohesively. Subsequently suggesting that for natural scientists what can be explained is more important than what cannot be explained for science to advance. On the contrary, what cannot be explained in the natural world is what drives the force of science to seek out and research new information. Without first identifying the unknown there would be nothing to explain. An example which suggests that what can be explained is more important than what cannot be explained in the natural sciences is the autobiography “Brain on Fire” by Susannah Cahalan. Cahalan developed a rare neurological autoimmune disease known as anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis. This illness affected the part of Cahalan's brain that controlled thoughts, mood and movements, displaying psychiatric symptoms, seizures, and memory loss. Most cases with this illness are sent to a psychiatric asylum, as scientists are unable to understand the medical prognosis.

However, the unknown cause of her illness led doctors to research new theories utilising trial and error to find out the probable causes, seeking out a way to explain her symptoms. A doctor theorised after numerous testing through the scientific method that it was an autoimmune disease. The gaps in the knowledge of what the doctors understood about the disease led to them inquiring about new research with new methods to diagnose by making the patient draw a clock because the abnormal handwriting of a clock that the patient exhibits identified what was happening neurologically. Therefore, it is important for scientists to identify what cannot be explained to allow them to dive deeper into the unknown to uncover new discoveries. Although, once one understands where and what to research, what can be explained in the research is more important to allow science to advance.

Additionally, the acknowledgement of what we can't explain only sometimes needs to be present to acquire new data for natural scientists. In the natural sciences, it is extremely important to explain the unknown, however, the unknown isn't always sought out but found as a product of searching for another discovery. Therefore, what cannot be explained is not important in some circumstances, such as the serendipitous discovery of cyclins, taught in my IB biology class. Cyclins were discovered as a byproduct of research by Tim Hunt and his team when researching protein synthesis in eggs. Throughout this research, the team recognised a protein, cyclins which would decrease or increase in concentration in certain phases of the cell cycle. This discovery provided new research findings that cyclins control the process of the cell cycle. Although serendipitous discoveries are not frequent, they provide evidence that what can be explained is more important than what cannot be explained as scientists do not need to identify the unexplained to acquire new information.

To conclude, what can be explained and what cannot be explained are both quintessential parts of the natural science and art process. Although for artists it can be argued that what cannot be explained is more important, because if all art is restricted by the context of the piece or the intention of the artists then there is no room for the viewer's creative freedom. However, in some cases, the context surrounding art is essential to understand the entire impact of the piece, although not all art requires a context or intention. Unlike artists, for natural scientists, it can be argued that what can be explained is more important than what cannot be explained. The identification of the unknown allows science to expand its knowledge, but what we can explain forms the backbone of natural science. The discoveries found in the natural sciences are useless unless they can be explained and further applied. Although in some cases the unknown is found without being sought out, suggesting that it's not always important.
